Ask what time is and a picture arrives before any answer does: a
river we float down, a line we move along, a container everything
sits inside. The picture is so old and so useful that it feels like
perception. It isn't. It is an image, and most of what puzzles us
about time is really what puzzles us about the image.

• • • —

Start somewhere smaller than the universe. A musician doesn't have
time; she keeps it. A breath keeps its own time through the taking,
the turning, the release. A conversation keeps time in the way its
silences and answers pace one another. A mountain keeps time too,
so slowly we call it timeless, which only means its rhythm is not
ours. Watch any happening closely and you find not a thing sitting
in time but an act keeping it, with a pace, a rhythm, a while that
belongs to that happening and no other.

This is the claim, and it is simpler than the river: a happening
does not occur in time, because its happening is its time. [[tending|To
tend]], to reach out of what has been, with what accompanies,
toward what calls, is not something done inside a temporal
container. The happening is the time. "Was," "is," and "will be" are
how acts sound from inside other acts. The words are real, but
there is no clock outside all the happenings for them to point to,
only happenings keeping time with happenings.

Then what about the line, the timeline, the biography, history
marching left to right? Here is where this account differs from the
usual rebellions against clock-time: the line is real. It is just
local. A story told, a plan laid, a calendar kept, a life
remembered in order are genuine achievements, made by beings who
share time and care where it is going. Sequence is something we do
together, and do well. The mistake was never the line. The mistake
was the promotion, which crowned a local human instrument as the
form of time itself, so that inheritance becomes "behind us," hope
becomes "up ahead," and everything real is strung on one thread it
never asked to hang from.

But if there is no line, doesn't everything come loose? Can't we go
back? No, and the reason is the deepest fact this work knows: every
act happens [[once|once]]. What completes does not come back.
Nothing can un-condition what conditioned it, and no happening can
be its own ancestor. That is irreversibility, and notice that it
never needed a line. One-way facts everywhere; no path anywhere.
"Before" is ancestry, not an address.

And the clocks? The caesium atom, the atomic second, the
astonishing agreement of instruments across the world? Clocks are
not windows onto the container. They are coordination technology,
rhythms so widely braided that any local ensemble can couple to
them, the way an orchestra tunes to an oboe. The oboe is not the
music's master; it is the habit everyone can hear. Every happening
participates in many tempos at once, its cells' and its
household's and its civilization's, nested rhythms with no master
beat. The time is held between us, around here, by nobody and
everybody.

Even the famous flow, time's river-feel, the slipping of it,
survives this account, relocated. Nothing flows; tendings tend, and
some of them feel it. Beings like us, who keep long time together,
thicken their keeping into memory, anticipation, the lived arrow.
The flow is real the way music is real, as an achievement of
ensembles, not a current under the world.

So: what is time? The word names no thing. It is the family-name
for happenings keeping time together, the map-word for all the
braided rhythms, and a good word so long as the map is not mistaken
for the territory. If you want the territory, it is nearer than any
definition: the next breath, kept; the conversation, paced; the
once of this very reading, already becoming what some later act
will tend out of.

Listen to a room for a minute. Count nothing. There are more times
in it than clocks in the world, and they are, all of them, keeping
time.
