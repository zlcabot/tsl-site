Anna Lowenhaupt Tsing is an American anthropologist whose work examines capitalism, landscape, multispecies life, and the unstable collaborations through which worlds continue. _The Mushroom at the End of the World_ follows matsutake mushrooms through disturbed forests, migrant livelihoods, commodity chains, science, and histories of war and extraction.

• • • —

## Collaborative survival

Tsing's collaborative survival is not harmony. Beings coordinate without sharing one purpose. Their relations can be enabling, exploitative, accidental, or destructive.

Contaminated diversity refuses the fantasy of untouched nature. Life emerges through histories whose damage cannot simply be removed. Salvage accumulation names capitalism's capacity to turn value produced in noncapitalist relations into commodities.

This prevents multispecies entanglement from becoming politically innocent. Collaboration happens inside uneven systems of labor, ownership, and appropriation.

## The reading here

Tsing gives [[sympoiesis]] empirical and political thickness. A forest, commodity chain, and mushroom ecology coordinate without a central conductor and without becoming one harmonious organism.

Her arts of noticing are also important to [[tending]]. Large claims must remain answerable to local histories, livelihoods, disturbances, and mismatched times. Tsing's ethnography keeps relational philosophy from floating above the damaged places it describes.

Tsing and [[donna-haraway]] are collaborators and interlocutors, not a simple teacher and successor. Their relation is best approached as a shared conversation transformed through different methods.

Begin with [_The Mushroom at the End of the World_](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691220550/the-mushroom-at-the-end-of-the-world).
