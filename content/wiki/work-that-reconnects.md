The Work That Reconnects is a body of group practices developed principally by [[joanna-macy|Joanna Macy]] and carried by an international facilitator network. It draws on engaged Buddhism, systems thinking, deep ecology, ritual, and activism to help participants remain available to ecological and social crisis.

• • • —

## The spiral

The practice is commonly arranged as a spiral:

1. gratitude;
2. honoring pain for the world;
3. seeing with new or ancient eyes;
4. going forth.

The spiral gives pain a relational meaning. Grief is possible because attachment and participation are real. Held collectively, it can widen rather than narrow the field of response.

Active hope does not require confidence that a preferred future will occur. It names chosen participation in bringing that future closer.

## The reading here

The Work That Reconnects is the most developed communal practice in this contemporary cluster. It gives grief, gratitude, widened perception, and renewed action a repeatable social form.

The spiral should remain a practice architecture, not a universal sequence. Some grief does not open into action. Some action begins before emotional integration. Some situations require rest, refusal, or defense.

A practice can tend the conditions of transformation without producing transformation on schedule. That is where it meets [[tending]] most closely.
