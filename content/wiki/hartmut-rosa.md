Hartmut Rosa is a German sociologist and critical theorist whose work examines modernity through acceleration, alienation, resonance, and the drive to make the world controllable. He asks why technologies that save time coexist with deepening temporal scarcity, and why societies in constant motion can feel unable to change their form of life.

• • • —

## Acceleration and standstill

Rosa distinguishes technological acceleration, the acceleration of social change, and the acceleration of the pace of life. Modern institutions increasingly depend on growth and innovation merely to reproduce themselves. He calls this dynamic stabilization.

The result is frenetic standstill. More events, choices, communications, and innovations occur, yet the underlying social form remains fixed. Permanent urgency becomes one of modernity's ways of staying the same.

## Resonance

Rosa's later work asks what a non-alienated relation to the world would be. Resonance involves being affected, answering in one's own voice, and being changed by a relation that cannot be manufactured or guaranteed.

The desire to make everything visible, reachable, usable, and controllable undermines this possibility. A world that can answer is a world that can also surprise, resist, or remain unavailable.

## The reading here

Rosa supplies an important diagnosis for [[tending]]. Urgency can be real, but a permanent regime of urgency may suppress the perception and coordination that an adequate response requires.

Tending overlaps resonance without making it the universal norm. Conflict, refusal, completion, asymmetry, and silence may also be real forms of relation. The question is not whether the world resonates with us, but how multiple happenings keep time together and what their coordination permits or prevents.
