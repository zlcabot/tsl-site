Sympoiesis means making-with. The term names worlds and systems produced collectively through relations that no participant controls alone. It is often contrasted with autopoiesis, self-production, especially when self-production is imagined as the work of an autonomous and closed unit.

• • • —

## Haraway's use

In _Staying with the Trouble_, [[donna-haraway|Donna Haraway]] uses sympoiesis to describe multispecies becoming, ecological response, and artistic-scientific world-making. Humans do not make worlds alone. Companion species, technologies, landscapes, histories, and institutions participate in the processes through which capacities and boundaries emerge.

This gives sympoiesis an ethical force. If beings become with others, response is not a moral relation added after independent selves already exist. Response-ability develops within the relations that make action possible.

## The reading here

Sympoiesis and [[tending]] are close, but they are not synonyms. Sympoiesis emphasizes collective production. Tending also asks how an occurrence inherits, holds with others, reaches, completes, and becomes available to later acts.

Making-with should not be romanticized. Co-production includes parasitism, coercion, extraction, predation, and asymmetric dependence. A relational description does not by itself tell us whether a relation should be preserved.

That is the decisive practical question: what is being made together, who can answer, whose boundaries are possible, and what forms of completion are being prevented?
