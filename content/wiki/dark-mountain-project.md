The Dark Mountain Project is a cultural movement founded by [[dougald-hine|Dougald Hine]] and Paul Kingsnorth. It began with an uncivilisation manifesto and developed through books, gatherings, visual art, performance, and essays.

Its question is cultural: what becomes possible when faith in perpetual progress, human centrality, and technological control no longer organizes imagination?

• • • —

## Uncivilisation

Uncivilisation does not simply celebrate collapse. It attempts to step outside the stories by which industrial civilization presents itself as inevitable and universally desirable.

Art becomes a way of noticing beings, losses, places, and historical scales that the progress narrative excludes. Cultural work continues inside damage rather than waiting for a clean beginning after modernity.

## The reading here

Dark Mountain matters to [[tending]] because stories do more than represent a world. They coordinate attention, identity, action, and possibility. When a story no longer holds, replacing its message is not enough. The habits of relation it organized must also change.

Narrative interruption does not by itself provide a politics. Withdrawal from progress can open ecological perception, or it can slide toward fatalism and political ambiguity. The constructive question remains: what can be learned, made, repaired, defended, completed, or released here?
