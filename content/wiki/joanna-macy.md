Joanna Macy is an American scholar and activist whose work brings together Buddhist practice, systems thinking, deep ecology, anti-nuclear activism, and group process. She is the principal developer of the [[work-that-reconnects|Work That Reconnects]], a communal practice for facing ecological and social crisis without collapsing into denial or apathy.

With Chris Johnstone, _Active Hope_ presents hope as something enacted rather than predicted. A person can choose participation without knowing that the preferred future will arrive.

• • • —

## Grief and action

The Work That Reconnects often moves through gratitude, honoring pain for the world, seeing with new or ancient eyes, and going forth. Grief is not treated as a private malfunction. It discloses attachment and can reopen participation when it is held collectively.

Macy places this work within a possible Great Turning from an industrial growth society toward a life-sustaining society. The image gives direction and courage, but it can also sound like a promised historical transition.

## The reading here

Macy's work is important to [[tending]] because it gives grief a social and practical form. Pain can become available to further response without being denied or glorified.

The sequence should not become a universal psychology. Some grief does not culminate in action. Some action must begin before grief is integrated. A practice can prepare conditions for transformation without producing the outcome on schedule.
