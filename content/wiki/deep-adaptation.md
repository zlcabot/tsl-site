Deep Adaptation is a framework and network associated with [[jem-bendell|Jem Bendell]]. It developed from the possibility of severe climate-related social disruption and asks what people and communities might sustain, release, restore, repair, reclaim, and regenerate when familiar expectations of progress no longer guide planning.

• • • —

## The six questions

- Resilience: what capacities and values should endure?
- Relinquishment: what should be released to reduce harm or free capacity?
- Restoration: what older practices and relations should return?
- Reconciliation: what harms, deaths, and divisions require reckoning?
- Reclamation: what agency, land, knowledge, or power should be recovered?
- Regeneration: what forms of life and relation can be renewed?

The questions became more than an academic framework. They generated forums, networks, courses, and community practices organized around grief, preparation, mutual aid, and meaning.

## The reading here

Relinquishment is Deep Adaptation's most important contribution to [[tending]]. Care can require deciding what should not continue.

The sequence should not become a law of history. Different communities and ecologies may be sustaining, relinquishing, restoring, and collapsing at the same time. Regeneration is possible, but it is not the promised reward for loss.

The empirical probability and timing of societal collapse also remain contested. The practical value of the questions should be separated from the scientific strength of a particular forecast.
