Jenny Odell is an American artist and writer whose work moves among ecology, technology, labor, attention, and public space. Her writing begins from experiences that are widely familiar: captured attention, permanent busyness, temporal scarcity, and the sense that every moment must become productive.

_How to Do Nothing_ proposed refusal-in-place rather than withdrawal from the world. _Saving Time_ extends the argument from attention to the social and political construction of clock time.

• • • —

## More than one time

Odell does not need to deny clocks to place them. Standardized time is a powerful coordination technology shaped by histories of labor, commerce, transport, and control. Its apparent universality can hide bodily rhythms, ecological cues, geological scales, seasonal changes, and unequal power over whose time counts.

Attention is therefore not merely a private mental resource. What becomes perceptible changes what can solicit response. Birds, tides, transit systems, work schedules, neighborhoods, and damaged landscapes disclose different relations when someone learns how to notice them.

## The reading here

Odell gives the public discussion of [[time]] an immediate doorway. The line and clock are real achievements. The mistake is promoting one local instrument into the form of time itself.

The reading here makes a further claim: happenings do not merely occupy different amounts of one time. Their happening is their time, and shared time is something their rhythms accomplish together.

Odell's work protects this claim from abstraction by keeping labor, place, ecology, and daily perception in view. It also warns against romanticizing ecological time as an innocent nature outside history. Ecosystems are disturbed, engineered, inherited, and changing too.

Begin with [_Saving Time_](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/672377/saving-time-by-jenny-odell/).
