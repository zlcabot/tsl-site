Dougald Hine is a British writer, educator, and cultural organizer who co-founded the [[dark-mountain-project|Dark Mountain Project]] with Paul Kingsnorth. His work asks what forms of storytelling, learning, and collaboration become possible when the modern stories of progress and control no longer convince.

Dark Mountain's uncivilisation project invited writers and artists to step outside the assumption that industrial modernity is inevitable, universally desirable, or capable of solving every consequence of its own expansion.

• • • —

## Working in ruins

Ruins name both damage and location. Cultural work does not begin after a clean civilizational reset. It begins among inherited institutions, broken promises, surviving practices, and forms of life that continue without the old story of progress.

Hine's later educational projects and _At Work in the Ruins_ carry this question into learning, science, and climate response. He is an important companion to [[vanessa-machado-de-oliveira]], whose work also asks how modernity can be accompanied without being granted another triumphant future.

## The reading here

Hine's strongest contribution is cultural rather than metaphysical. Stories actively coordinate attention, identity, and possibility. When a story fails, changing its content is not enough. The habits of perception and relation it organized must also be tended.

The danger is that collapse can become another total story. The work remains local and constructive: what can still be learned, made, repaired, completed, or released here?

Begin with [_At Work in the Ruins_](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/800906/at-work-in-the-ruins-by-dougald-hine/9781645022800/).
