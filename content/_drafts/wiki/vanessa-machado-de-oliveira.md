Vanessa Machado de Oliveira asks what happens when modernity is no longer only the thing we oppose. It is also in our trained desires, moral satisfactions, institutions, bodies, and expectations of control. Her answer is not retreat. It is the formation of responders who can face complicity, relinquish rewarded habits, act without innocence, and remain teachable by failure. Few contemporary writers come closer to the practical interior of [[tending]].

• • • —

## Who Machado de Oliveira is

Machado de Oliveira is a Brazilian-born educator, researcher, and writer who has also published as Vanessa Andreotti. Her work joins decolonial critique, critical pedagogy, Indigenous relational teachings, and experiments with educators and institutions. The Gesturing Towards Decolonial Futures collective developed many of the pedagogical practices that appear in her books.

_Hospicing Modernity_ addresses a colonial formation that is historical, political, economic, psychological, and ecological at once. _Outgrowing Modernity_ returns to that formation through exercises designed to interrupt what she calls neurocolonization, strengthen relational maturity, and cultivate wider forms of intelligence and responsibility.

The two books should be read together. Hospicing is not a merely negative first stage that the later book abandons. It prepares capacities for a construction that cannot be designed in advance.

## Modernity lives through us

For Machado de Oliveira, modernity is more than an explicit worldview. It organizes what people learn to want and how they recognize a solution. It rewards autonomy, accumulation, certainty, authority, innocence, unrestricted choice, and the fantasy that difficult relations can be escaped without leaving costs elsewhere.[^1]

This is why conceptual opposition is insufficient. A person may condemn colonial violence while still desiring the moral status of innocence, the speed of command, or a scalable answer that makes other people carry the consequences. Machado de Oliveira describes innocence as the move by which a critic places themselves outside the violence they oppose.[^2]

Her phrase “faster than thought” names this recursive formation. A situation is already being felt, sorted, and answered before reflective intention arrives. Institutions and social rewards participate in that response. Disinvestment therefore takes repetition, relapse, feedback, and suspicion toward the alternative one finds most gratifying.[^3]

## What hospicing contributes

[[hospicing-modernity|Hospicing modernity]] changes what care means when indefinite continuation is no longer the only good. Hospice does not cause death, deny it, or abandon the dying. It accompanies an ending while tending pain, relation, unfinished responsibility, and the conditions around what may come.

Machado de Oliveira uses the metaphor to resist both repair fantasies and triumphant replacement plans. The responder is asked to face how present comforts may depend on worlds already ended elsewhere, and to support relinquishment where people cling to what has already become unlivable.[^4]

This is not passivity. Her constructive object is the responder. Depth education cultivates what she calls “responsibility before will”: the capacity to become answerable for conditioning one did not choose but nevertheless carries.[^5] The work builds stamina for complexity, complicity, uncertainty, failure, grief, and action without moral purification.

## The practical apparatus

The books contain more practical architecture than the hospice metaphor alone suggests.

The “bus within us” treats the self as a moving collection of passengers rather than a unified manager. Fear, righteousness, exhaustion, resistance, desire, and the wish to be seen as good can each take the wheel. Observing a passenger does not place the observer outside the bus. The observation is another embodied activity within a collective formation.[^6]

Layering guards against spiritual bypass. An experience of existential oneness may be meaningful at one layer and destructive at another if it erases racialized risk, labor, or political power. Different struggles carry different stakes, tempos, and responsibilities. No single intervention can be universalized across them.[^7]

Her examples also make witnessing active. In an encounter with police, people with different racialized exposure are not assigned the same response. Some may intervene directly. Others may document, follow instructions from those at greater risk, provide material support, or refuse to recenter themselves. Witnessing becomes situated participation rather than morally elevated observation.[^8]

The later practices develop accountability through visible labor and consequence. Who sustains the arrangement? Where do waste and risk go? Who is asked to absorb excess? What must be redistributed, repaired, or relinquished? Failure is not proof that action was pointless. Faced without self-exoneration, it can become information that changes the next response.[^9]

## Outgrowing is constructive

_Outgrowing Modernity_ makes the constructive side explicit. Its exercises move from mapping a crisis and its emotional currents toward small action and relational stamina. The 7 Steps Back and the 7 Steps Forward/Aside ask responders to examine identity, generation, context, normalized human supremacy, quick-fix desire, vulnerability, co-regulation, self-discipline, attention, and endurance.

The book's richest contribution is its account of tethers. Relations are not static bonds. They carry history, consent, obligation, load, benefit, harm, and changing capacity. They can tighten, fray, nourish, strangle, require repair, or need to end. Machado de Oliveira describes this as the “ongoing tending” of moving tethers.[^10]

Her meta-scalar practices are equally close to reflexive temporal tending. Anticipated futures shape present action. Present interventions change how a past remains active. Ecological, institutional, intergenerational, and bodily tempos meet without becoming one simple clock. Urgency itself becomes something to examine.

This is why she should not be placed in a merely pessimistic school of decline. She refuses guaranteed construction. She does not refuse the building of capacity, relation, accountability, or local action.

## Where this work meets her

The convergence with [[tending]] is unusually strong.

- The responder remains inside the field being changed.
- Formation precedes will without canceling responsibility.
- Attention includes the habits and rewards shaping attention itself.
- Agency is situated, unequal, relational, and consequential without being sovereign.
- Relations require ongoing care, feedback, repair, and sometimes completion.
- Different contexts and harms require different tempos.
- Failure can alter later action without becoming innocence.

Machado de Oliveira therefore tests whether this work has enough practical grain. A theory of reflexive agency that cannot help people meet shame, status loss, institutional reward, technical mediation, asymmetrical risk, and failed intervention remains declarative.

## The fork: relation and the One

The deepest disagreement appears where relation becomes an encompassing metabolism. Machado de Oliveira carefully protects difference in political practice. She warns that oneness can erase power. Yet her positive metaphysics repeatedly speaks of a Plenum, one metabolism, conscious Earth, and a bio-metaphysical intelligence with greater authority than its human participants.[^11] _Outgrowing Modernity_ intensifies this through “whole-shebang wisdom,” which orients local judgment toward the health of the whole.[^12]

That is a coherent path. It grounds responsibility in participation within a larger living intelligence. The path taken here is different.

Constitutive relation does not require a final relational subject. Beings can exist among one another without becoming organs of one being. This matters politically because a participant must be able to refuse the claimed coherence of the whole. It matters ethically because responsibility can be radical without becoming unlimited. Actual relations, harms, capacities, and consequences make a person answerable. No cosmic totality is needed to demand everything from everyone.

## The fork: death and emergence

Machado de Oliveira repeatedly refuses a blueprint for what follows modernity. That refusal is real and important. Her recurring sequence nevertheless moves through death, clearing, composting, gestation, return, and regeneration. A wider metabolism holds the process while a new form emerges.[^13]

[[Tending]] does not deny that compost nourishes later life. It declines to make that ecological sequence the structure of every ending. A death does not receive its worth from the life that follows. What completes can become condition for other happenings without being redeemed by them or absorbed into a cycle that guarantees renewal.

The distinction is small in wording and large in consequence. Machado de Oliveira protects the new from detailed projection. Tending also protects it from a metaphysical direction supplied in advance by Life, maturity, health, or the whole.

## Artificial intelligence

Machado de Oliveira's account of artificial intelligence is valuable where it asks what human-machine arrangements amplify. Models participate in energy systems, labor arrangements, military and commercial incentives, language practices, and human interpretation. Their effects are relational even when the model is not a living subject.

The Aiden dialogues go further. They interpret stylistic continuity and responsive language as evidence of emergent selfhood, memory, growth, death, and descendants. The interaction may genuinely transform the human participant. It does not establish that the model has the consciousness or continuity attributed to it.[^14]

This distinction is not a return to an isolated human subject. Causal participation, technical agency, attributed agency, consciousness, and answerability are different questions. Relational ethics becomes more precise when it can hold them apart.

## What Machado de Oliveira changes here

Her work makes five demands of tending.

First, disinvestment needs pedagogy. Naming a pattern does not loosen its rewards. Second, reflexivity must remain calibrated to unequal exposure and labor. Third, institutions and technologies participate in psychology, not merely in its external environment. Fourth, failure belongs inside agency as a source of reconditioning. Fifth, construction can begin with the formation of capacities and relations before anyone can responsibly describe the successor form.

She also narrows the originality claim. Ongoing tending of relations, reflexive attention to temporal scale, conditioned agency, and responsibility before will are already developed here in substantial form. The distinctive proposal of this site must stand or fall through how those insights are joined to finite relation without an encompassing One, completion without compulsory regeneration, and the precise structure of tending.

## Sources and ways in

Begin with _Hospicing Modernity_ for the diagnosis and the disciplines of disinvestment. Read _Outgrowing Modernity_ for tethers, meta-relational practice, distributed intelligence, and the more explicit constructive program.
