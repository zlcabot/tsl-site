Vanessa Machado de Oliveira is a Brazilian-born educator and writer whose work joins decolonial critique, critical pedagogy, Indigenous relational teachings, and practical work with institutions. Her central question is not simply how modernity might be criticized. It is how people formed by modernity might cease reproducing it through their desires, habits, innocence claims, and expectations of control.

Her books _Hospicing Modernity_ and _Outgrowing Modernity_ approach modernity as a colonial formation that lives through more than laws and economies. It also lives through the demand to be autonomous, certain, morally pure, unrestricted, and entitled to accumulation.

• • • —

## From hospice to outgrowing

Hospice changes the meaning of care. A hospice does not abandon the dying, cause the death, deny it, or promise a cure. It asks what care becomes when indefinite continuation is no longer the only good.

Applied to modernity, the metaphor calls for expanded capacity to face pain, complicity, complexity, uncertainty, and failure. It also calls for disinvestment from the desires through which colonial modernity persists inside those trying to oppose it.

_Outgrowing Modernity_ develops this work through strategic disinvestment and four capacities: sobriety, maturity, discernment, and responsibility. It also turns toward reinvestment in relations and structures that value life beyond accumulation.

## The reading here

Machado de Oliveira is a major neighbor for [[tending]] because she refuses the fantasy that the responder stands outside the problem. A response carries its own habits, satisfactions, exclusions, and timing into what it addresses.

Her work also sharpens an essential point: tending is not synonymous with preservation. Care may require helping a form complete rather than extending its life at any cost.

The open question is how construction follows disinvestment without becoming another project of mastery. That question must be answered through the books themselves, not through the hospice metaphor alone.
