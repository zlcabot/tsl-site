Seraphic Group matters because it attempts something philosophy often postpones. It turns a vision of relation into companies, products, media, technology, agricultural work, and investment. That ambition deserves to be taken seriously. It also makes the project answerable in ways a cosmology alone is not.

• • • —

## What Seraphic is

Seraphic Group presents itself as an innovation hub developing and scaling “root-cause solutions” for human health. Its public site identifies [[zach-bush|Zach Bush]] as founder and places ION Intelligence of Nature at the center of its current health work.[^1]

Bush's wider Biological Renaissance now describes a larger ecology. Experience, health, community, energy, storytelling, intelligence, and governance appear as seven systems within “one living organism.”[^2] Named expressions include _Biological Elegance_, educational programs, ION, Farmer's Footprint, Project Biome, the Institute of Natural Law, media initiatives, AI tools, land projects, industrial resource systems, and capital vehicles.

Not all of these initiatives are necessarily owned, financed, or governed by one legal entity. The public philosophical architecture is broader than the legal Seraphic company. That difference should stay visible. “Seraphic” can refer narrowly to a corporation, while the Biological Renaissance names a network of organizations and projects with different institutional forms.

## Why the institutional attempt matters

Relational philosophy is easy to admire while it remains an account of the world. Institutions force it to decide.

Who owns the product? Who evaluates the evidence? Who carries liability? How is capital allocated? Which community can refuse? What happens when ecological benefit, financial return, founder vision, and local consent point in different directions? How does an organization learn that its own theory of health is mistaken?

Seraphic is therefore more than background to Bush. It is a live experiment in whether a philosophy of participation can organize material practice. The attempt includes real constructive work. Farmer's Footprint uses story and education to move public attention toward soil and regenerative agriculture. The Institute of Natural Law tries to connect ecological restoration, social relation, economics, and governance. The intelligence projects foreground privacy and energy use as design questions rather than afterthoughts.[^3]

The presence of commerce does not invalidate those efforts. Products, wages, land, infrastructure, laboratories, software, and media require resources. The question is how the relations created by that commerce are governed and corrected.

## One organism and the problem of coherence

The network's central image is one organism with differentiated organs. The image promises coherence without treating every activity as an unrelated brand. It gives health products, agriculture, AI, capital, and storytelling a shared answer to the question of what they are for.

The same image can conceal institutional structure. Legal entities, ownership, fiduciary duties, research conflicts, decision rights, and affected communities do not disappear because they participate in one story. Organic language can make central coordination feel natural and render disagreement as a failure of coherence.

This is the political question inside the metaphysics. Who speaks for the organism?

[[Tending]] offers a plural alternative. Institutions can share purposes and remain deeply interdependent without becoming organs of one subject. Coherence is then a revisable achievement among participants, not a property interpreted from the center. Refusal and dissent remain possible sources of information rather than signs of pathology.

## Five evidence tracks

Seraphic and the wider network are easiest to assess when five tracks remain distinct.

### Philosophy

The philosophical claim is that health and intelligence arise through embodied relation rather than isolated mechanism. This claim can be examined through argument, experience, and comparison with other relational philosophies.

### Ecology

Claims about soil, food, microbiomes, water, agriculture, carbon, and ecosystem restoration require ecological and biological evidence appropriate to their scale. A soil-health result does not establish a clinical effect in a person.

### Clinical efficacy and safety

Claims about digestion, inflammation, permeability, immunity, gluten response, or disease require human evidence, safety systems, and proportionate public language. Mechanistic plausibility is not clinical efficacy.

### Institution and governance

Claims about privacy, distributed authority, community benefit, ecological alignment, or responsible capital require visible policies and practice. Founder intention and public philosophy do not establish governance.

### Commerce

Pricing, ownership, intellectual property, marketing, investment, and financial incentives affect what gets studied and said. Commercial interest does not make a claim false. It makes disclosure and independent correction necessary.

Keeping these tracks separate prevents two opposite errors. A person should not accept a health product because the ecological philosophy is moving. Nor should one regulatory failure be used as proof that every philosophical or agricultural claim is false.

## ION and the science-to-sales bridge

ION's science materials describe a proprietary lignite extract, Terrahydrite, as a signaling complex rather than a conventional nutrient. The site attributes effects involving tight junctions, reactive oxygen species, microbiome diversity, hydration, immunity, lysine, and collagen.[^4]

The two principal peer-reviewed papers are in vitro. They study intestinal cell models exposed to glyphosate or gluten and report effects on measures associated with tight-junction integrity.[^5] These are relevant mechanistic studies. They do not establish symptom relief or clinical benefit in humans.

The company also presents small human reports using urinary biomarkers over a short period. These studies can generate questions. They do not carry the weight of independent clinical trials with meaningful outcomes. A lower urinary glyphosate measurement, for example, is difficult to interpret as reduced intestinal absorption because urinary glyphosate is an exposure biomarker and human absorption and excretion remain uncertain.[^6]

The issue is not that commercial research should be ignored. The issue is the distance between what a study measured and what a customer is invited to expect.

## The regulatory record

The institutional test became concrete in 2023 and 2024. FDA testing found gross microbial contamination in an ION nasal spray. Biomic Sciences voluntarily recalled all lots of ION Sinus Support, ION Sinus, and Restore Sinus Spray. FDA warned that the route of administration created a high risk of harm for vulnerable patients.[^7]

The May 2024 warning letter identified Zachary Bush as owner of Biomic Sciences. It documented failures involving microbial testing, quality-unit oversight, component identity and contamination specifications, finished-product specifications, quarantine, batch documentation, calibration, food-process registration, and labeling. FDA also found several company responses insufficient or unsupported at the time of the letter.[^8]

The public material reviewed for this page did not reveal a later FDA close-out letter. The absence of a located record is not evidence that the company undertook no correction. It means the public page should not imply regulatory resolution that has not been verified.

The recall and warning are not marginal to a philosophy of relational health. Identity, purity, contamination, documentation, quality authority, and correction are the practical relations through which a product becomes safe enough to enter another body.

## What tending asks of an institution

Seraphic clarifies that institutional tending requires more than a compelling purpose.

- A claim must remain revisable by evidence that can resist the founder's story.
- Research and marketing must state the distance between mechanism, biomarker, symptom, and clinical outcome.
- Quality systems must sense what a product actually contains and how it changes over time.
- Conflicts and ownership must remain visible where they shape judgment.
- Affected communities and workers need standing to answer the institution.
- Dissent must be able to change practice without first becoming aligned with the whole.
- Correction must become public enough for trust to be earned again.

These are not bureaucratic burdens imposed on living relation. They are forms of memory, attention, intention, and response through which an institution remains answerable.

## What Seraphic changes here

Seraphic prevents [[tending]] from remaining a philosophy whose successes are conceptual and whose failures are always elsewhere. The work must eventually specify organizations, products, technical systems, land relations, and capital. It must also accept that institutional embodiment will expose contradictions the philosophy could hide in prose.

The experiment additionally sharpens a warning about “life” as an ethical authority. Living systems include symbiosis and mutual aid, but also predation, exclusion, parasitism, and collapse. No desired organization follows simply from calling itself biological. Ethical form has to be argued and tended among the actual participants.

The constructive wager remains worth keeping. Philosophy should try to become practice. Seraphic shows why that attempt needs an architecture of correction as developed as its architecture of coherence.
