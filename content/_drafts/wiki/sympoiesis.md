Sympoiesis means making-with. The term names collective production in systems whose spatial and temporal boundaries are not fully self-defined and whose control is distributed. M. Beth L. Dempster proposed it in 1998 as a contrast to autopoietic systems understood as self-producing, bounded, centrally controlled, and comparatively predictable. [[donna-haraway|Donna Haraway]] made the term central to multispecies thought in _Staying with the Trouble_.[^1]

• • • —

## Haraway's radical use

For Haraway, sympoiesis is more than a systems category. It is a challenge to bounded individualism. Organisms do not first exist and then enter environments or partnerships. Cells, microbes, hosts, ecologies, technologies, and histories make one another's capacities and provisional boundaries.

Her holobiont is not one harmonious superorganism. It is a contingent knot of heterogeneous participants. Symbiosis can include benefit, predation, dependence, indifference, and harm. The point is not that relations are good. The point is that no actor is self-made.[^2]

Sympoiesis also changes ethics. Response is not added between completed individuals. Response-ability is cultivated inside the relations through which beings become capable of answering at all.

## The encounter with tending

Sympoiesis and [[tending]] share a refusal of self-production and a commitment to plural, situated happening. They are nevertheless not synonyms.

Sympoiesis identifies constitutive making-with. Tending offers an anatomy of an occurrence. Every happening extends out of completed acts, intends with other happenings in a shared while, and attends toward its own completing. Sympoiesis clarifies that none of this is solitary. Tending clarifies that making-with happens through finite acts whose temporalities and completions are not interchangeable.

This difference becomes especially useful when a relation changes. Sympoiesis can describe how participants co-produce a form. Tending asks whether the form should continue, alter, complete, release, or be refused, and how that response will condition what follows without controlling its arrival.

## The harder problem: making-with is not innocence

Co-production includes coercion, parasitism, extraction, colonization, and asymmetric dependence. A mine and a community make a landscape together, but not with equal power or benefit. A bureaucracy and an applicant co-produce an administrative event, but only one may control the timetable and categories.

Relational description must therefore be followed by discriminating questions:

- What is being made?
- Who or what has been rendered capable of answering?
- Whose boundaries and continuities are being protected?
- Which participants can leave, refuse, or alter the relation?
- What harms are being distributed as the cost of the whole?
- What needs to complete rather than be sustained?

These questions do not restore an isolated judge. They are themselves situated acts within the relation.

## What the encounter changes

Sympoiesis prevents tending from treating an event as a privately bounded unit. The event's boundary is an accomplishment with histories and participants, not a starting fact.

Tending, in return, adds completion and reflexivity to the public use of sympoiesis. The slogan “making-with” can otherwise sound like indefinite continuation. A finite act can make-with faithfully and still finish. A conscious act can also include its own way of making-with among what it tends, exposing how its care, categories, and desired outcomes participate in the field.

## Sources and ways in

Haraway's chapter “Sympoiesis” in _Staying with the Trouble_ is the primary entrance. The chapter also preserves the distinction she takes from Dempster and places it in conversation with symbiogenesis, holobionts, art, science, and damaged ecologies.
