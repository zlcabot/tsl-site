Donna Haraway is an American feminist theorist and historian of science whose work has reshaped feminist epistemology, science and technology studies, animal studies, and multispecies thought. Across the cyborg, situated knowledges, companion species, and the Chthulucene, she contests two linked fantasies: that knowledge can come from nowhere and that a being can make itself.

Haraway matters here less as the source of one doctrine than as the maker of a different grammar. Knowing becomes a material participation for which the knower is accountable. Beings become through relations rather than entering relations already complete. Response becomes a capacity cultivated with others. Stories and concepts become interventions in the worlds they describe.

## Staying inside the damaged relation

_Staying with the Trouble_ begins by refusing two symmetrical escapes from ecological devastation. One is confidence that technology or providence will rescue the world. The other is the conviction that the game is already over. Both leap from the damaged present toward a settled future, one repaired and one foreclosed. Haraway remains with living and dying in what she calls a thick present.[^1]

This staying is not patience with injustice. It is an active refusal of innocence. The present contains colonial histories, capitalist extraction, multispecies dependence, extinction, technical possibility, and unfinished obligation. No clean actor arrives from outside it. Haraway's compact statement, “We become-with each other or not at all,” makes dependence constitutive rather than optional.[^2]

The force of the thick present is temporal as well as ethical. Inheritance and possibility do not sit on opposite sides of a vanishing now. They are active together in the encounter. The question is therefore not how to leave trouble behind but how particular beings can become more capable of response within it.

## Sympoiesis and response-ability

[[sympoiesis]] means making-with. Haraway draws the term from M. Beth L. Dempster's contrast between self-producing systems and collectively producing systems whose boundaries, information, and control are distributed. Haraway radicalizes the term: living worlds are partnered all the way down. Organisms, microbes, technologies, institutions, histories, and landscapes participate in one another's formation.[^3]

This is not a claim that everything is harmoniously connected. Haraway's holobionts are contingent knots, not organic wholes. Symbiosis can include benefit, indifference, predation, and harm. Relations attach and detach. String figures are passed, altered, dropped, and taken up. Making-with includes cuts as well as ties.

Response-ability follows from this account. It is not merely blame assigned to an already independent moral subject. It is the cultivated capacity to notice and answer within relations that make every participant capable of some actions and not others. Haraway's use of Vinciane Despret is crucial here: beings can render one another capable of acts that were not available before the encounter.[^4]

That is Haraway's central gift to this work. Agency is neither possession nor isolated initiation. It is relationally elicited capacity with consequences.

## Where this work meets Haraway

The convergence with [[tending]] is deep.

- Nothing happens alone.
- Every account is situated and consequential.
- The real answers back rather than waiting passively to be represented.
- Capacity emerges through relations rather than belonging to a sealed individual.
- Description participates in world-making.

Yet this is a structural kinship, not an identity. Haraway's sympoiesis foregrounds co-production. Tending asks a further set of temporal questions: how does an act extend out of completed acts, intend with other happenings in a shared while, attend toward its own completing, and become what later acts tend out of? Haraway gives an extraordinarily rich account of becoming-with. Tending adds a more explicit anatomy of each happening and its completion.

The difference matters when response becomes reflexive. For Haraway, response-ability is cultivated in situated relations. In this work, [[consciousness]] is a tending that includes its own tending among what it tends, and [[agency]] is that fold at the human scale. The responder can therefore tend not only a damaged field but also the timing, habits, exclusions, and inherited purposes through which the response is being made.

## The political pressure

Haraway is more politically discriminating than a slogan such as “everything is connected” would suggest. She repeatedly asks who lives, who dies, how kinship is organized, and which ties must be made or cut. Her examples involve colonialism, extraction, reproductive justice, Indigenous struggle, scientific practice, and multispecies survival.[^5]

Still, a real pressure remains. Relational sensitivity does not by itself decide among rival interventions. Making-with describes coercive institutions as accurately as regenerative ecologies. Response-ability can name the capacity to answer, but political action also requires judgments about power, obligation, refusal, protection, and force.

Tending does not solve those questions by supplying a universal rule. It does, however, block one evasion. Distributed agency does not entail evenly distributed responsibility. Participants differ in power, alternatives, knowledge, and the harms they impose or inherit. A relation may be constitutive and still need to be interrupted. A world may be co-produced and still need to end in its present form.

## What Haraway changes here

Haraway prevents tending from becoming a private ethic of careful attention. If tending is real, it must include microbes, infrastructures, scientific instruments, political histories, and the beings whose capacity to answer has been suppressed. She also prevents completion from becoming hygienic closure. What completes leaves inheritances in a damaged world, and later acts arise through those remains.

She leaves a durable test: can this framework discriminate among ways of making-with without reinstalling a sovereign judge outside the relation? The answer cannot be given once. It has to be enacted in the specificity Haraway requires.

## Sources and ways in

_Staying with the Trouble_ is the most direct entrance to sympoiesis, response-ability, and the thick present. “Situated Knowledges” supplies the epistemological background, and _The Companion Species Manifesto_ shows how the argument changes when worked through concrete interspecies histories.
