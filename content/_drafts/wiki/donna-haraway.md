Donna Haraway is an American feminist theorist and historian of science whose work has transformed feminist epistemology, science and technology studies, animal studies, and multispecies thought. Her writing repeatedly challenges the fantasy of a neutral view from nowhere and the equally powerful fantasy of an autonomous being that makes itself.

Situated knowledge begins from accountability for the material and historical position through which knowing occurs. The cyborg and companion species writings extend this insight: humans become through technologies, animals, microbes, ecologies, and political histories.

## Staying with the trouble

In _Staying with the Trouble_, Haraway refuses two familiar stories about ecological devastation. One promises that innovation will rescue the world cleanly. The other announces that the game is already over. Haraway asks instead how beings can become capable of response while living and dying together on a damaged Earth.

[[sympoiesis]] means making-with rather than self-making. No being or world produces itself alone. Making kin and response-ability name the obligations that arise within those relations.

Her thick present is not an isolated moment of contemplation. It holds inherited damage, ongoing attachment, unfinished obligation, and possible composition together.

## The reading here

Haraway is the hinge between the essays on dying modernity and altered response. She offers ongoingness without compulsory preservation, and compost without resurrection.

This work is close to [[tending]], but the terms have different jobs. Sympoiesis emphasizes collective production. Tending also asks how an act inherits, holds with others, reaches, completes, and becomes available to later acts without guaranteeing what comes next.

The political pressure point remains important. Situated response must still distinguish care from capture, reciprocity from extraction, and generative delay from delay that protects power.

Begin with [_Staying with the Trouble_](https://www.dukeupress.edu/staying-with-the-trouble).
