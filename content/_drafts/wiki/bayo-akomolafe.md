Báyò Akómoláfé is a Nigerian-born psychologist and writer whose work joins Yoruba worlds, decolonial thought, ecopsychology, process-relational philosophy, and more-than-human politics. His characteristic figures are cracks, monsters, fugitivity, fermentation, exile, and sanctuary. They interrupt the stable world and sovereign agent presumed by conventional problem-solving.

• • • —

## Postactivism

[[postactivism]] does not mean that activism is obsolete or that passivity should replace it. The word names moments when the inherited arrangement of problem, solver, goal, and solution no longer fits.

Its central question is recursive: what if the way a problem is framed and solved helps reproduce the problem? Urgency may narrow perception to familiar levers. Expertise may preserve the fantasy that the world waits passively for human intervention. Slowing down can interrupt this reflex so that excluded actors, histories, and possibilities become perceptible.

Making sanctuary is not the discovery of a safe place outside crisis. It is a practice of composing with grief, brokenness, strangeness, and more-than-human participation inside damaged worlds.

## The reading here

Akómoláfé is one of the closest contemporary neighbors to reflexive temporal [[tending]]. Both refuse an agent standing outside the field of response. The responder is formed and changed through the response.

Tending nevertheless does not make slowness or the crack intrinsically superior. A burning room, a coercive institution, a wounded body, and damaged soil solicit different timings. The question is what keeps time with what, who bears the mismatch, and what the response itself is making possible.

Distributed agency also does not mean evenly distributed responsibility. Postactivism must eventually answer the cases where direct intervention is necessary and delay protects power.

Begin with Akómoláfé's essay [“What I Mean by Postactivism”](https://www.bayoakomolafe.net/post/what-i-mean-by-postactivism).
