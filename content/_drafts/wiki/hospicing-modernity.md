Hospicing modernity is the practice and metaphor developed by [[vanessa-machado-de-oliveira|Vanessa Machado de Oliveira]] for facing the limits of colonial modernity without denial, abandonment, or another fantasy of mastery.

A hospice does not cause death or promise cure. It changes what care means when indefinite continuation is no longer the only good.

• • • —

## What is being hospiced

Modernity is more than a collection of institutions. It also lives through desires for autonomy, certainty, innocence, accumulation, unrestricted choice, and moral authority. People may oppose colonialism conceptually while remaining attached to the satisfactions and problem-solving reflexes colonial modernity has trained.

Hospicing therefore includes expanding the capacity to face pain, complicity, uncertainty, and failure. It also includes disinvestment from the desires through which the formation persists.

## What hospice does not mean

Hospicing is not passivity. End-of-life care remains skilled, active, relational, and time-sensitive. Nor does the metaphor mean that everything associated with modernity should be discarded. Rights, sciences, infrastructures, and emancipatory institutions can be real achievements even when the larger formation has become destructive.

There is also no single patient on one schedule. Different institutions, communities, and ecologies may require defense, transformation, relinquishment, or completion.

## The reading here

Hospicing clarifies why [[tending]] is not synonymous with maintenance. Care can include helping a form end.

What follows remains open. An ending does not guarantee a wiser successor. The work is to tend the conditions of possible arrival without turning the future into another civilizational plan.
