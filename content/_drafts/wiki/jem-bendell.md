Jem Bendell is a British scholar and public writer associated with sustainability, collapse discourse, and [[deep-adaptation|Deep Adaptation]]. His work became influential by asking people and institutions to consider not only prevention and mitigation, but how they might live under the possibility of severe social disruption.

Deep Adaptation is currently organized around six questions: resilience, relinquishment, restoration, reconciliation, reclamation, and regeneration. Its most important contribution to this project is relinquishment. Responsible care may include deciding what should not be preserved.

• • • —

## The reading here

Bendell makes release a practical category rather than a spiritual mood. What capacities should endure? What harmful forms should end? What older relations should be restored? What injuries require reconciliation? What agency should be reclaimed? What can regenerate?

These questions are useful without treating the sequence as a guaranteed path. Collapse can become a master future that organizes every present act. The reading here begins instead from plural [[time|times]] and local conditions. Different communities and ecologies are not living on one civilizational schedule.

Bendell's forecasts and uses of climate science have also been contested. The value of relinquishment should be distinguished from the empirical probability and timing of any particular collapse scenario.
