Postactivism is [[bayo-akomolafe|Báyò Akómoláfé]]'s name for ruptures in which established modes of political response lose their fit. It is not the period after activism, a recommendation for passivity, or a new program claiming to supersede previous movements. It is the interruption that occurs when problem, solver, method, goal, and measure of success can no longer continue unchanged.

Akómoláfé describes it as what becomes possible when “things no longer fit.”[^1] The phrase matters because postactivism is not first an ideology one adopts. It names an event in the arrangement through which action had made sense.

## When the solution belongs to the problem

Postactivism asks a recursive question: what if a response reproduces the world that generated the harm?

A climate movement may oppose extraction while retaining a fantasy of planetary management. A development project may seek justice while deciding in advance whose knowledge counts. A campaign may win recognition by becoming legible to the institution it opposes. An emergency response may centralize authority, narrow perception, and make the exceptional measure permanent.

None of these possibilities means action is futile. They mean that action has no innocent exterior. The activist, institution, tool, deadline, and desired outcome are participants in the situation, not neutral instruments applied to it.

## Cracks, sanctuary, and slowness

The crack is postactivism's central figure. It marks a break in continuity through which excluded actors and possibilities become perceptible. Bodies, infrastructures, microbes, weather, ancestral inheritances, accidents, and nonhuman beings can change what the problem is and who is capable of responding.

Slowing down can help a crack remain open. It can interrupt the speed with which an institution turns uncertainty into a familiar plan. But slowness is tactical, not sacred. Its point is altered availability.

Sanctuary names a related practice. It is not an untouched zone beyond crisis. It is a composition made within damage where beings can become otherwise with one another. Sanctuary is therefore provisional, situated, and politically vulnerable. It can shelter fugitivity without pretending to be outside the forces from which beings flee.

## The encounter with tending

Postactivism and [[tending]] converge in refusing a sovereign agent. A response is one happening among the happenings it changes. When response becomes reflexive, it can include its own framing, timing, desire, and consequence among what it tends.

Tending makes three additions explicit.

First, the issue is not slowness but timing. A rapid act can be responsive, and a slow process can be violent. Second, relational participation does not equal moral responsibility. Power, alternatives, and exposure remain uneven. Third, interruption is only part of an act's anatomy. A crack extends out of inheritances, holds participants together in a new tension, and tends toward forms of completion that may themselves need to be examined.

## The direct-harm test

Postactivism becomes politically credible where it can distinguish four different refusals of speed:

- slowing an extractive process so damaged beings can answer;
- slowing the powerful so excluded participants can enter;
- delaying protection while harm continues;
- refusing a false deadline that exists to close deliberation.

These are not equivalent. The same language of pause can name solidarity, strategy, bureaucracy, or abandonment.

The direct-harm test asks: who is being asked to wait, who benefits from the wait, what can be repaired later, and what will become irreversible? A situated answer may require immediate intervention. Postactivism should change the quality and self-understanding of that intervention, not prohibit it in advance.

## What the encounter changes

Postactivism corrects any tendency to present reflexive agency as superior command. An agent who includes its own action in the field has not escaped implication. It has become more exposed to surprise, contradiction, and transformation.

Tending, in return, presses postactivism toward a fuller account of completion and responsibility. A rupture is not valuable merely because continuity failed. The question is what the interruption makes possible, what it prevents, which inheritances it carries, and how its consequences become available to later acts.

## Sources and ways in

Akómoláfé's “What I Mean by Postactivism” is the clearest short entrance. _These Wilds Beyond Our Fences_ supplies the figures of the middle, cracks, monstrosity, re-turn, and compost that make the term intelligible.
