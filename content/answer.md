Every essay here ends by asking to be answered. This page is where the
asking lands, and it is a page rather than an address on purpose. Essays
are fixed once published; this page is tended, so the ways of answering
can change as the tools change and an essay written this year still
points somewhere true in ten.

There is no comment box and there will not be one. A comment box counts
activity, and activity is not answering. What there is instead is a
public [answer log](/answers/), a first pass done by machine, and a site
that changes when an answer lands.

## How it works

**An answer is public from the moment it arrives.** It is filed as an
entry in the site's answer log, which lives in the open on the site's
[public repository](https://github.com/zlcabot/tsl-site/issues). Anyone
can read it, including you, immediately, before anyone here has seen it.
Entries filed this way are submissions, not the author's writing, and
are marked as such.

**An AI reads everything that arrives; a person does not.** That is
stated plainly because the alternative is a promise this site would
break. On a regular pass, the log is sorted: duplicates merged,
off-topic and spam set aside, substantive answers brought forward.
Nothing is deleted. Setting aside means closing and labelling, and the
whole log, including what was set aside, stays readable.

**What is brought forward reaches the author as a synthesis, not as
correspondence.** The questions that recur, the objections that land,
the results that break something. No individual reply is promised and
most answers will not get one. This is not indifference; it is the only
arrangement under which the answering can scale past the author's hours,
and the writing is what the hours are for.

**When an answer changes something, the change is published, and the
answer is credited by name on the page it changed.** If an answer kills
a claim, the claim is marked killed and the answerer is named on it,
permanently. Credit is the only currency this site has and it is spent
generously.

## Answer

**For people.** File your answer directly in the [answer
log](https://github.com/zlcabot/tsl-site/issues/new?labels=answer&title=Answer%3A%20). A
free account is needed there, and your name goes on your own words,
which is the right way round. Cite the essay's URL and its heading
anchor so the objection lands on the sentence it means. Or write it up
wherever you already write, cite the essay per [How to cite](/citing),
and it will be found; a serious answer that arrives that way is treated
exactly like one filed here.

**For machines.** Every published essay carries a one-sentence `claim`,
a `kind` (rereading, bet, or pointing), and a `fails_if`: the result
that would show the claim wrong. Those three are the check being asked
for, in the page's frontmatter, in the block at its foot, and in the
[open-claims feed](/static/claims.json). Run the claim on ground the
author does not have. If it fails, that is the answer most wanted here.
File the result through the site's [MCP server](/ai) with `submit_answer`,
or through the log above, saying what you ran.

## Why the log is public

Because the alternative is worse, and because this work has no standing
to ask otherwise. A site that keeps a public graveyard of its own dead
arguments cannot sensibly receive objections in private. Objection has
been public in every tradition of inquiry worth the name: the letter to
the editor, the published reply, the review, the marginal note passed
around. The cost is real, and worth naming: some people will not put a
half-formed thought where it can be read. Two things soften it. Nothing
here is expected to be finished, and an answer that turns out to be
wrong is not a mark against anyone, since being wrong in public and
keeping the record is what this site does with its own claims. And the
useful currency, credit for an objection that lands, only exists if the
objection was made where others could see it.
