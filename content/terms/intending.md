---
title: Intending
description: Stretching with — the relational direction; the membrane, the between, the holding of difference.
date: 2026-08-08
---

*In-tendere*: to stretch in, to stretch within. Intending is the
second direction of [[tending]] — the stretch **with**.

First, a reclamation. In everyday English, *intending* has narrowed to
mean having a plan — "I intend to call you." That is a late,
specialized descendant. The older, structural sense is the one this
work restores: the stretching that happens *between* and *within* —
the holding-together of things that remain different.

The best picture of it is the living cell's membrane. A membrane is
not a wall and not an opening; it is the holding of an inside *with*
an outside, each kept different, each kept in relation, neither
collapsing into the other. Life begins there — not in the stuff on
either side, but in the holding. Or take a real conversation: not your
words, not mine, but the *between* that makes them answers to each
other. That between is not nothing, and it is not a third thing
standing alongside us. It is intending — the with, stretched and held.

Intending has a characteristic failure: **fragmentation**. The with
lets go, and what was held-together-in-difference falls into pieces
that no longer answer each other — the conversation that becomes two
monologues, the community that becomes a crowd.

Its health is the rarest of skills: **holding difference without
dissolving it** — with-ness that does not require sameness.

Intending is one of three directions, and it needs the other two:
without [[extending]] and [[attending]] there is nothing distinct to
hold, and nowhere for the holding to reach. See [[tending]] for the
whole.
