---
title: Attending
description: Stretching toward — the caring direction; attention as a form of care.
date: 2026-08-08
---

*Ad-tendere*: to stretch toward. Attending is the third direction of
[[tending]] — the stretch **toward**.

English remembers what this word carries. An attendant is one who
cares. A physician attends. We attend to a child, to a guest, to a
dying friend. Attention, before it was a cognitive resource to be
measured and monetized, was a form of care — the stretch toward what
calls for it.

Attending is directedness without a fixed destination. It is not a
plan, not a goal, not a schedule — those are attending frozen into
extending's forms. It is the leaning of a happening toward what
matters to it: the sunflower's slow turn, the ear finding the one
voice in the room, the care that orients a whole life without ever
being written down.

Attending has a characteristic failure: **fixation**. The toward
seizes on one object and cannot release — the grip that mistakes
itself for care, the vigilance that has forgotten what it guards.
Which reveals the deep thing about attending's health: it is as much
**release** as grasp. Real attention can let go. Care that cannot
release its object has stopped being care and become possession. The
highest form of the toward is an open one — directed, warm, and
unclenched.

Attending is one of three directions, and it needs the other two:
without [[extending]] there is nothing formed to move toward anything,
and without [[intending]] the toward has no with to travel in. See
[[tending]] for the whole.
