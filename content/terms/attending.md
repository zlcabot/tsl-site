Attending is [[tending]] toward. It names the arc of an act, the
way every happening orients, faces, and curves toward its
completing. A crystal grows in a direction. An orbit holds its arc.
A thought curves toward its object. This is directionality itself,
before any plan, any purpose, any watcher, and at the human scale
it flowers as care.

• • • —

## The word

*Ad-tendere*: to tend toward. This is the third way of [[tending]],
and English keeps the caring flower of it everywhere: the
attendant, the attending physician, attending to a child, a guest,
a dying friend. It also keeps the spent version, attention as
something paid.

## What it names

Attending names the structural towardness of every act. It is not a
consciousness observing from outside and not a goal already set; it
is the act's own orientation, intrinsic to its completing. The
crystal is not aware of its growth-direction, and needs no
awareness for the direction to be real. Directionality comes first.
Minds are one place it flowers, not where it begins.

At the human scale, attending becomes the thing we know most
intimately, which is care: the leaning of a life toward what calls
it, the ear finding the one voice in a full room, the care that
orients decades without ever being written down. One guard is worth
stating. Attending is not "the future." The toward is now, because
direction is something an act does, not a place it is headed.

## The failure

Attending's characteristic failure is fixation. The toward seizes
one object and cannot release: the grip that mistakes itself for
care, the vigilance that has forgotten what it guards. Which
reveals the deep thing about attending's health, that it is as much
release as reach. A toward that can let go of any particular target
without losing direction, staying directed and warm and unclenched,
is attending at its fullest. Care that cannot release its object
has stopped being care and become possession.

## Relations

One of three ways of one act. Without [[extending]] there is
nothing formed to have an arc, and without [[intending]] the arc
has no with to travel in. The whole is at [[tending]].
