Attending is [[tending]] **toward** — the arc of an act: the way every
happening orients, faces, and curves toward its completing. A crystal
grows in a direction. An orbit holds its arc. A thought curves toward
its object. Directionality itself, before any plan, any purpose, any
watcher — and at the human scale, this towardness flowers as care.

• • • —

## The word

*Ad-tendere*: to tend toward. The third way of [[tending]]. English
keeps the caring flower of it everywhere — attendant, attending
physician, attending to a child, a guest, a dying friend — and keeps
the spent version too: attention, *paid*.

## What it names

Attending names the structural towardness of every act — not a
consciousness observing from outside, not a goal already set, but the
act's own orientation, intrinsic to its completing. The crystal is not
*aware* of its growth-direction, and needs no awareness for the
direction to be real. Directionality comes first; minds are one place
it flowers, not where it begins.

At the human scale, attending becomes the thing we know most
intimately: care. The leaning of a life toward what calls it — the
ear finding the one voice in a full room, the care that orients
decades without ever being written down. And a guard worth stating:
attending is not "the future." The toward is now — direction is
something an act does, not a place it is headed.

## The failure

Attending's characteristic failure is **fixation**: the toward seizes
one object and cannot release — the grip that mistakes itself for
care, the vigilance that has forgotten what it guards. Which reveals
the deep thing about attending's health: it is as much release as
reach. A toward that can let go of any particular target without
losing direction — directed, warm, unclenched — is attending at its
fullest. Care that cannot release its object has stopped being care
and become possession.

## Relations

One of three ways of one act: without [[extending]] there is nothing
formed to have an arc, and without [[intending]] the arc has no with
to travel in. The whole is at [[tending]].
